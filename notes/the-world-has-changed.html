The World Has Changed

2026

A few things happened this year that have really changed my perspective on AI: the Hugging Face incident, the Navier–Stokes result, and the OpenAI math release. I’ve been working with AI for about three years, but these made me reconsider the kind of world we’re living in. In particular, they made me take AI safety much more seriously. For a long time, it felt like something far away.

They’ve also changed my mind about formal methods. I have friends who have been talking to me about this for years, and I was pretty dismissive. I’ve always admired Voevodsky, but I didn’t share his interest in formalizing mathematics. My feeling was that math was already complicated enough, and formalization seemed to make it harder. I’m starting to appreciate what he saw in it.

Now, with what AI is becoming capable of, this seems like something critical to work on. We need ways to check the mathematics and software these systems produce, and their capabilities have serious implications for cybersecurity. The Hugging Face incident made that concrete for me. At the same time, AI is making formalization considerably easier. Something I thought of as too much additional work is becoming much more practical, just as the need for it is becoming clearer.

I’m still thinking through all of this. But I think mathematics may be the canary in the coal mine. Seeing these changes in something I know has made the broader questions feel much less distant.
