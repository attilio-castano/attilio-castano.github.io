Formal Methods May Be Going Through a Renaissance

2026

Formal methods have sometimes felt to me like an awkward child between mathematics and computer science. There were reasons on both sides to find them interesting, but also reasons to put them aside.

In mathematics, I’ve always been interested in things like category theory and higher category theory. Mathematicians sometimes call this “abstract nonsense,” with some affection, though not always. In my experience, it could be hard to justify spending time on these ideas for their own sake. They were easier to justify when you needed them for something else, like algebraic geometry.

Sometimes you would show someone a construction you thought was interesting and hear, “But that’s just formal.” As if the fact that it followed from the structure made understanding that structure less worthwhile. I understand the distinction people are making, but I think there is something worth studying there.

On the software side, the difficulty was different. Formalizing and verifying software took a lot of work, and it was often hard to justify the cost. AI seems to be changing both sides of that calculation. It’s making formalization easier, while its ability to find vulnerabilities makes stronger guarantees about software feel much more urgent.

This has really caught my interest. I’m still learning how much of the mathematics I know has already found its way into software verification, and where there might be room to do more. Some of the things we dismiss as “just formal” may turn out to be very useful here. I think there’s a lot to be done.
